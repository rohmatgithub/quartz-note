# Valuation

This note collects frameworks for estimating value and understanding what price already implies.

## Methods

- DCF
- Multiples
- Sum-of-the-parts
- Scenario analysis
- Reverse valuation

## What Good Valuation Looks Like

- Anchored in business economics, not spreadsheet complexity
- Sensitive to assumptions that actually drive value
- Explicit about what the current price already assumes
- Framed as a range, not a false point estimate

## Core Questions

- What are the main value drivers?
- Which assumptions matter most?
- What does the current market price imply?
- Where can you be directionally right without precision theater?
- What downside exists if the thesis is wrong?

## Minimum Toolkit

- Revenue growth assumptions
- Margin path assumptions
- Reinvestment needs
- Cost of capital logic
- Bear, base, and bull cases

## Common Mistakes

- Treating model detail as investment insight
- Ignoring balance-sheet risk
- Using peer multiples without understanding comparability
- Failing to connect valuation to thesis and timing
- Underestimating how much expectations are already in price
