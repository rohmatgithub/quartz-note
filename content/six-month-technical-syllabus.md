# 6-Month Technical Syllabus

This syllabus is designed to build the technical base for public-markets investing. The goal is not to collect knowledge. The goal is to become capable of producing credible research, valuation work, and investment memos.

## How To Use This Syllabus

- Study 5-6 days per week
- Spend at least 60 percent of time on finance and investing material
- Convert everything you learn into notes, summaries, or memo sections
- End each month with one concrete output
- Use [[memos/first-stock-pitch-workspace|First Stock Pitch Workspace]] as the running applied project

## Month 1: Financial Statement Fluency

### Objective

Learn how the three financial statements connect and how to read them without confusion.

### Topics

- Income statement structure
- Balance sheet structure
- Cash flow statement structure
- How net income flows into cash
- Working capital mechanics
- Capex, depreciation, and free cash flow
- Debt, liquidity, and solvency basics

### Technical Skills To Build

- Read one annual report and identify the three statements
- Reconcile profit, operating cash flow, and free cash flow
- Explain why a company can show profit but weak cash generation

### Weekly Work

- Week 1: learn statement structure and major line items
- Week 2: study cash flow statement and working capital
- Week 3: study capital intensity, debt, and liquidity
- Week 4: summarize one real company annual report

### Output

- One note added to [[topics/finance/accounting-financial-statements|Accounting & Financial Statements]]
- One annual report summary
- One short explanation of statement linkages in your own words

## Month 2: Earnings Quality And Accounting Judgment

### Objective

Move beyond reading statements into judging the quality of reported economics.

### Topics

- Revenue recognition basics
- Non-recurring adjustments
- Stock-based compensation
- Working capital red flags
- Accruals and earnings quality
- Return on invested capital
- Margin quality and operating leverage

### Technical Skills To Build

- Detect weak cash conversion
- Separate accounting presentation from business economics
- Spot common red flags in management-adjusted earnings

### Weekly Work

- Week 1: revenue recognition and adjustment logic
- Week 2: cash conversion and accrual warning signs
- Week 3: margin analysis and return metrics
- Week 4: apply a checklist to a second real company

### Output

- Expanded checklist in [[topics/finance/accounting-financial-statements|Accounting & Financial Statements]]
- One red-flag case note
- One comparison note between two companies

## Month 3: Valuation Foundations

### Objective

Learn how to think about value, expectations, and price without hiding behind spreadsheet complexity.

### Topics

- Intrinsic value concept
- DCF mechanics
- Trading multiples
- Enterprise value versus equity value
- Terminal value
- Sensitivity tables
- Reverse valuation
- Bear, base, and bull cases

### Technical Skills To Build

- Build a simple valuation range
- Explain what a stock price implies about growth and margins
- Compare DCF logic with market-multiple logic

### Weekly Work

- Week 1: DCF inputs and value-driver logic
- Week 2: multiples and comparables
- Week 3: reverse DCF and expectation analysis
- Week 4: value one company with base, bear, and bull cases

### Output

- Expanded note in [[topics/finance/valuation|Valuation]]
- One valuation exercise
- One reverse-DCF note tied to a real company

## Month 4: Business Analysis And Thesis Construction

### Objective

Connect accounting and valuation to actual business quality and thesis formation.

### Topics

- Business model analysis
- Unit economics
- Industry structure
- Competitive advantage
- Management quality
- Capital allocation
- Cyclicality
- Variant perception

### Technical Skills To Build

- Explain how a business makes money and why it may sustain returns
- Connect business quality to valuation assumptions
- Write a thesis with clear upside, downside, and thesis-break conditions

### Weekly Work

- Week 1: business model and revenue drivers
- Week 2: unit economics and competition
- Week 3: management and capital allocation
- Week 4: write the first full thesis draft

### Output

- First serious draft in [[memos/first-stock-pitch-workspace|First Stock Pitch Workspace]]
- One business-quality framework note
- One industry-structure note

## Month 5: Portfolio Construction And Risk

### Objective

Learn how good single-name ideas translate into portfolio decisions.

### Topics

- Position sizing
- Concentration versus diversification
- Correlation and exposure overlap
- Drawdown and downside
- Risk/reward asymmetry
- Thesis-break conditions
- Liquidity awareness
- Process versus outcome

### Technical Skills To Build

- Explain why a position deserves a certain size
- Distinguish high-conviction from high-risk
- Review mistakes without hindsight bias

### Weekly Work

- Week 1: position sizing logic
- Week 2: diversification, concentration, and correlation
- Week 3: drawdown and risk framing
- Week 4: review one hypothetical or paper portfolio

### Output

- Expanded note in [[topics/finance/portfolio-construction-risk|Portfolio Construction & Risk]]
- One portfolio review note in [[portfolio-review|Portfolio Review]]
- Revised conclusion section in [[memos/first-stock-pitch-workspace|First Stock Pitch Workspace]]

## Month 6: Market Context, Memo Quality, And Research Tooling

### Objective

Raise the quality of judgment, writing, and workflow so the research process starts to look professional.

### Topics

- Rates and inflation basics
- Discount-rate sensitivity
- Macro regime awareness
- Memo writing quality
- Evidence hierarchy
- Research process discipline
- Python for finance workflows
- Watchlists, screens, and automation

### Technical Skills To Build

- Explain how macro affects multiples and sector behavior
- Write a cleaner memo with better evidence and less vague language
- Build one small research tool that saves time or improves consistency

### Weekly Work

- Week 1: macro basics and discount rates
- Week 2: rewrite memo sections for clarity and evidence quality
- Week 3: build one simple Python or spreadsheet workflow
- Week 4: finish the first complete memo and review the process

### Output

- One complete stock pitch
- One memo revision note
- One tool or workflow note under [[topics/learn-tech|Quant & Research Tech]]

## Technical Material Checklist

By the end of six months, you should be comfortable with:

- Reading income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement
- Judging earnings quality and cash conversion
- Using DCF, multiples, and reverse valuation at a basic but credible level
- Explaining business quality and competitive position
- Writing a structured stock pitch
- Framing risk, position sizing, and thesis-break conditions
- Using basic Python or spreadsheet tooling to support research

## Minimum Standard For Progress

- You can read a 10-K without getting lost
- You can explain the cash-flow implications of earnings
- You can defend a valuation range with explicit assumptions
- You can write a 2-5 page memo with clear risks
- You can explain why a position should be owned, watched, or rejected
